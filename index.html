How the 5-Year Rule Works

Personal finance planners often point to five years as the minimum time you should plan to stay in a home before buying makes financial sense. The reason comes down to two numbers: transaction friction and mortgage amortization.

On day one, buying a property involves upfront closing costs—origination fees, inspections, legal work, and transfer taxes or stamp duty—typically totaling 2% to 5% of the purchase price. When you sell, agent commissions and closing fees take another 5% to 7% off the sale price. If you move after two or three years, your home rarely appreciates enough to cover that 8% to 10% round-trip cost.

Meanwhile, early mortgage payments are heavily front-loaded toward interest. During the first five years of a 30-year loan at 6.5%, roughly 80% of your monthly principal-and-interest payment goes to the lender rather than your own equity. By year five, steady appreciation and rising principal payments usually start catching up—which is why running a rent vs buy 5 year calculator before making an offer saves buyers from costly guesswork.

What This Calculator Factors In

Comparing a monthly mortgage quote against a monthly rent check misses half the equation. This is it cheaper to rent or buy for 5 years calculator models the full balance sheet on both sides:

  • Unrecoverable Ownership Costs: Mortgage interest, annual property taxes, homeowners insurance, routine repairs, and both buying and selling closing costs. None of this money comes back to you when you sell.
  • Equity Buildup & Amortization: Each monthly payment reduces your loan balance based on an exact monthly amortization schedule. Combined with annual home appreciation, this determines your net proceeds after selling costs at the end of each year.
  • Opportunity Cost of Cash: Every dollar tied up in a down payment and closing fees is a dollar that cannot compound in the stock market or fixed-income assets. We calculate the investment gains a renter earns by keeping that same upfront cash invested over 5 years.
  • Rent Inflation: Unlike a fixed-rate mortgage, rent rarely stays flat. The model compounds your monthly rent annually so you can see how rising leases narrow the gap over time.

5-Year Rent vs. Buy FAQ

Why is 5 years used as the standard benchmark for renting vs. buying?
Buying and selling a home typically costs 7% to 10% of the property's value in combined transaction fees, taxes, and agent commissions. Over a 5-year period, steady home appreciation and mortgage principal paydown usually begin to offset those upfront and exit costs, making year five the common break-even window.
Does this rent vs buy 5 year calculator work for the UK, Canada, and Australia?
Yes. Switch the currency selector at the top to GBP, CAD, or AUD and adjust the local percentages. For example, UK buyers can fold Stamp Duty Land Tax into Buying Closing Costs, Canadian buyers can include Land Transfer Tax, and Australian buyers can enter state Stamp Duty and council rates.
How does opportunity cost affect the 5-year comparison?
When you buy a home, your down payment and closing costs are locked into real estate. If you rent instead, that cash can be invested in index funds, bonds, or high-yield savings. The returns you could have earned on that capital reduce the true net cost of renting.
What are unrecoverable costs when owning a home?
Unrecoverable ownership costs are expenses that do not build equity: mortgage interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, routine maintenance, and buying or selling closing fees. Comparing unrecoverable buying costs against rent gives a far more accurate picture than comparing a mortgage payment to monthly rent.
What happens if home prices stay flat or drop over the next 5 years?
Set the Annual Home Growth input to 0% or a negative number to stress-test your scenario. Without price growth, selling costs and early mortgage interest almost always make renting significantly cheaper over a 5-year window.
